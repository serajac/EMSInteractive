Domain 1 — ~18%
Airway, Respiration & Ventilation
If the patient can't breathe, nothing else matters. This domain is tested first in almost every scenario.

🫁 Airway Anatomy & Physiology

  • Upper airway: nares → nasopharynx → oropharynx → larynx (epiglottis = trapdoor)
  • Lower: trachea → carina → mainstem bronchi (right is more vertical) → bronchioles → alveoli
  • Cricoid = only complete tracheal ring; landmark for surgical airway
  • Normal adult RR: 12–20/min. Below 8 or above 29 = intervention required
  • Tidal volume ~500 mL. BVM delivers 500–600 mL per breath
  • Hypoxic drive in COPD: some patients rely on low O₂ to breathe — real concern, but NEVER withhold O₂ from a hypoxic patient

💨 O₂ Delivery Devices

  • Nasal cannula: 1–6 LPM → 24–44% FiO₂. Mild hypoxia, comfort.
  • Simple face mask: 6–10 LPM → 35–60% FiO₂. Minimum 6 LPM to flush CO₂.
  • Non-rebreather (NRB): 10–15 LPM → ~90% FiO₂. Reservoir bag must stay inflated.
  • BVM + O₂ @ 15 LPM: ~100% FiO₂. For apneic or RR <8.
  • CPAP: 5–20 cmH₂O. CHF/pulmonary edema — splints alveoli open between breaths.
  • Venturi mask: Precise FiO₂ (24–50%). Titrated O₂ for severe COPD.

🛠️ Airway Adjuncts

  • OPA: No gag reflex. Measure: corner of mouth to earlobe. Adults: curve or rotate 180°. Peds: straight with tongue blade.
  • NPA: Gag reflex present. Measure: tip of nose to earlobe. Bevel toward septum. Lubricate. Contraindicated in basilar skull fracture.
  • Basilar skull fracture signs: Raccoon eyes, Battle's sign (mastoid ecchymosis), CSF from ears/nose.
  • Suction: Yankauer for oropharynx. Max 15 sec adults, 10 sec peds. Suction while withdrawing.

⚠️ Respiratory Emergencies

  • Asthma: Bronchoconstriction + mucus. Expiratory wheezing, tripod. Assist MDI. O₂. Transport.
  • CHF/Pulmonary edema: Pink frothy sputum, crackles, orthopnea, JVD. Upright. CPAP. O₂.
  • Tension PTX: Absent unilateral BS + tracheal deviation (away) + JVD + hypotension. Seal 3 sides for open PTX. ALS for tension.
  • Epiglottitis: Drooling, tripod, "hot potato" voice. NO oropharynx exam. Blow-by O₂. Emergency transport.
  • Croup: Seal-bark cough, inspiratory stridor, toddlers. Cool humidified air. O₂. Transport.
  • Pulmonary embolism (PE): Sudden pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea, tachycardia, no wheeze. O₂. ALS. Rapid transport. (Cannot diagnose in field — suspect and treat.)
Domain 2 — ~20%
Cardiology & Resuscitation
Know Chain of Survival, shockable rhythms, all shock types, and AMI presentations including atypical.

🔗 Chain of Survival + CPR Standards

  • Early recognition → Early CPR → Early defibrillation → ALS → Post-resuscitation care
  • Compressions: ≥2" depth, 100–120/min, full recoil, minimize interruptions (<10 sec hands-off)
  • Basic airway (BVM only): 30:2 ratio. Advanced airway in place: continuous compressions + 1 breath every 6 sec
  • Resume CPR immediately after shock — do NOT check pulse first

⚡ AED Rules

  • Shockable: VF, pulseless VT. Non-shockable: Asystole, PEA → CPR + reversible causes (H's & T's).
  • Peds <8 yr/<55 lbs: pediatric pads or attenuator if available.
  • Water: dry chest. Patch: remove and wipe. Implanted device: offset pad placement.

💔 AMI — All Presentations

  • Classic: Crushing/pressure, radiation to arm/jaw/back, diaphoresis, N/V, doom.
  • Atypical (diabetics, women, elderly): indigestion, fatigue, dyspnea, epigastric pain — no chest pain.
  • Treatment: O₂ if SpO₂ <94% → 324mg aspirin chewed → assess nitro eligibility → ALS → transport.
  • Nitro contraindications: SBP <90, HR <50 or >100, PDE-5 inhibitors within 24–48 hrs, inferior MI with RV involvement.

😵 Shock — All 6 Types

  • Hypovolemic: Blood/fluid loss. Pale, tachy, diaphoretic. Supine, O₂, hemorrhage control.
  • Cardiogenic: Pump failure. JVD + crackles. Semi-Fowler's (NOT supine). CPAP. ALS.
  • Anaphylactic: Hives + stridor + hypotension. Epi auto-injector FIRST. O₂.
  • Septic: Infection + AMS + tachy + hypotension. O₂. ALS fluids. Rapid transport.
  • Neurogenic: Spinal injury. Bradycardia (NOT tachy) + warm flushed skin below injury + hypotension.
  • Obstructive: Tension PTX or tamponade (Beck's Triad: hypotension + muffled heart sounds + JVD).
Domain 3 — ~14%
Trauma
Pattern recognition drives trauma care. Master assessment tools, hemorrhage control, rapid transport decisions.

🔍 DCAP-BTLS

  • Deformities · Contusions · Abrasions · Punctures — Burns · Tenderness · Lacerations · Swelling
  • Apply head-to-toe on every trauma patient during secondary assessment

🩸 Hemorrhage Control

  • Step 1: Direct pressure — 3 full minutes, don't peek
  • Step 2 (extremity): Tourniquet — 2–3" above wound, tighten until bleeding stops, note time, do NOT remove
  • Step 2 (junctional — groin/axilla/neck): Wound packing + sustained pressure
  • Internal bleeding: recognize (distended/rigid abdomen, hypoperfusion, MOI) → TRANSPORT is treatment

🧠 TBI — Traumatic Brain Injury

  • Cushing's Triad: Hypertension + Bradycardia + Irregular respirations → rising ICP, impending herniation
  • Blown unilateral pupil = uncal herniation compressing CN III
  • GCS: Eyes (1–4) + Verbal (1–5) + Motor (1–6). Max 15. ≤8 = severe TBI.
  • Do NOT hyperventilate routinely — only in active herniation under medical direction
  • Airway, O₂, spinal precautions, rapid trauma center transport

🔥 Burns

  • Rule of Nines: Head=9%, each arm=9%, each leg=18%, ant. trunk=18%, post. trunk=18%, genitalia=1%
  • 1st degree: NOT counted. 2nd (partial): counted, blisters, painful. 3rd (full): counted, leathery, may be painless.
  • Inhalation injury signs: singed nasal hair, soot in airway, hoarse voice → AIRWAY emergency
  • Cool with room-temp water (no ice), dry dressing, keep warm, rapid transport

🦴 Musculoskeletal & Spinal

  • Traction splint: midshaft femur ONLY. Contraindicated: hip, knee, pelvic, lower leg fractures, partial amputation
  • Femur fracture: 1–1.5L blood loss internally → hemorrhagic shock
  • Pelvic fracture: wrap at greater trochanters. Massive internal blood loss possible.
  • Neurovascular check (PMS: Pulse, Motor, Sensation) before AND after splinting
  • Impaled object: stabilize in place, NEVER remove
Domain 4 — ~27%
Medical Emergencies
Largest domain. Your hospital background is a genuine advantage here.

🧠 AMS — AEIOU-TIPS

  • Alcohol/Acidosis · Epilepsy/Electrolytes · Insulin · Overdose · Uremia
  • Trauma/Temperature · Infection/ICP · Poison/Psych · Stroke/Seizure/Shock
  • First-time behavioral change with no psych history = medical emergency until proven otherwise

🩸 Diabetic Emergencies

  • Hypoglycemia (<70): Rapid onset, diaphoresis, tremors, seizure. Oral glucose if can swallow. If not → O₂ + transport for IV/IM glucose.
  • DKA: Gradual, Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath, polyuria/polydipsia. O₂, transport, ALS fluids.
  • Field rule: When in doubt, give glucose. Check BGL on every AMS patient.

🧠 Stroke — FAST + LKW

  • Facial droop + Arm drift + Speech abnormal + Time (Last Known Well)
  • 1 positive = suspected stroke. Transport to stroke center. Pre-notify.
  • Do NOT give aspirin (cannot rule out hemorrhagic). Check BGL (mimics stroke).
  • LKW time = treatment clock: tPA window 4.5 hrs, thrombectomy up to 24 hrs.

☠️ Toxidromes

  • Opioid: Miosis + respiratory depression + AMS. BVM FIRST, then Narcan.
  • Cholinergic (SLUDGE-M): Organophosphates/nerve agents. ALS antidote = atropine.
  • CO poisoning: Multiple patients + enclosed space + combustion appliance. SpO₂ falsely normal. High-flow NRB O₂ regardless.
  • Stimulant: Tachycardia, HTN, hyperthermia, mydriasis. Cool, calm, O₂, transport.

🌡️ Environmental Emergencies

  • Heat stroke: AMS + high temp. Rapid cooling = priority treatment. "Cool and transport."
  • Heat exhaustion: Normal temp, moist/cool/pale skin, alert. Cool, oral fluids if alert, transport.
  • Hypothermia: Core <95°F. Remove wet clothing, gentle handling, passive rewarm. "Not dead until warm and dead."

⚡ Seizures

  • Protect from injury, do NOT restrain, nothing in mouth
  • Status epilepticus: >5 min continuous OR no recovery between — ALS intercept (benzodiazepines)
  • Common causes: medication noncompliance, ETOH withdrawal, hypoglycemia, hypoxia, head trauma, eclampsia
  • First-time seizure: always transport — hidden cause must be ruled out
Domain 5 — ~11%
OB & Pediatrics
OB = two patients. Peds = not a small adult. Both tested with scenario-based questions.

🤰 Normal Delivery + OB Complications

  • Crowning: support head, check for nuchal cord (slip over if loose; double-clamp and cut if tight)
  • Deliver shoulders, dry/warm/stimulate neonate, clamp cord 6–8" from infant
  • Prolapsed cord: Moist dressings + elevate hips + do NOT push cord back + emergency transport (C-section)
  • Placenta previa: Painless bright-red bleeding. No vaginal exam. Transport.
  • Abruption: Painful dark-red bleeding, rigid abdomen. Rapid transport.
  • Eclampsia: Seizure in pregnant patient. Left lateral recumbent. O₂. ALS.
  • Ectopic: Unilateral lower quadrant pain + shock signs in early pregnancy. Rapid transport.
  • Supine hypotension: 3rd trimester IVC compression. Left lateral recumbent or wedge right hip.

Domain 6 — ~10%
EMS Operations
Scene safety, MCI triage, communications, legal duties. Easy points — easy to overlook during clinical studying.

🚨 Scene Size-Up — PENMAN

  • Personal safety/PPE · Environment/hazards · Number of patients
  • Mechanism/Nature · Additional resources · Next steps

🟥 START Triage — RPM

  • Step 1: Direct walkers → GREEN
  • Step 2 — R: Respirations. Absent after reposition = BLACK. >30/min = RED.
  • Step 3 — P: No radial pulse = RED.
  • Step 4 — M: Mental status. Cannot follow commands = RED. Otherwise = YELLOW.

⚖️ Legal & Ethical Essentials

  • Informed consent: Alert, competent adult understands procedure, risks, alternatives
  • Implied consent: Unconscious/incapacitated → EMT may act
  • Refusal: Competent adult's right. Explain risks, document, witness signature, leave contact info.
  • Abandonment: Cannot leave after initiating care without transferring to equal or higher provider
  • Negligence: Duty + Breach + Causation + Damages (all four required)
  • DNR: Must be written, physician-signed, dated. If in doubt → begin resuscitation + contact medical direction
New — Bonus Domain
📈 ECG Rhythm Recognition
EMTs don't interpret 12-leads, but you DO need to recognize AED-relevant rhythms and understand clinical correlations. Your hospital background makes this fast to learn.

🔋 Normal Sinus Rhythm (NSR)

  • Rate: 60–100 bpm. Regular. P wave before every QRS. PR interval 0.12–0.20 sec. QRS <0.12 sec.
  • The baseline to compare everything else against
  • Clinical: patient stable, no intervention needed based on rhythm alone
🧠 Hospital Analogy

NSR = the hospital's normal sinus rhythm on the monitor at the nurses' station. You've seen thousands. Everything else is a deviation from this — your job is to spot the deviations that require immediate field action.

⚡ Shockable Rhythms — Know These Cold

  • Ventricular Fibrillation (VF): Chaotic, no recognizable pattern. "Bag of worms" appearance. No pulse. AED → SHOCK. CPR until AED ready.
  • Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia (pVT): Wide, bizarre QRS complexes, rapid rate (>100), regular. No pulse. AED → SHOCK.
  • Both = no effective cardiac output = cardiac arrest = CPR + defibrillation
  • Key: ALWAYS confirm pulselessness before defibrillating — monomorphic VT with a pulse is NOT a field defibrillation situation

❌ Non-Shockable Rhythms — CPR Only

  • Asystole: Flat line. No electrical activity. "No shock advised." CPR + treat reversible causes. Confirm in 2 leads.
  • PEA (Pulseless Electrical Activity): Organized electrical activity on monitor + NO PULSE. Can look like normal sinus, sinus tach, or any organized rhythm. AED will say "no shock." CPR + identify and treat reversible cause.
  • PEA causes = Hs and Ts (ALS domain but good to know): Hypovolemia, Hypoxia, H+ (acidosis), Hypo/Hyperkalemia, Hypothermia, Tension PTX, Tamponade, Toxins, Thrombosis (PE/AMI)
🧠 Hospital Analogy

PEA = the ECG shows a rhythm but when you feel the wrist there's no pulse — like a heart that's sending electrical signals but the mechanical pump has failed. You've seen this post-arrest in the ICU. The electrical system is running; the pump is not. No electricity to shock. Fix the underlying cause.

📋 12-Lead Basics — What the EMT Needs to Know

  • 12-lead ECG is within scope for many systems — your hospital background is a major asset here
  • STEMI patterns: ST elevation in contiguous leads = transmural infarction. Tells the hospital which vessel is occluded.
  • Inferior MI (II, III, aVF): Right coronary artery (RCA). HIGH RISK of RV involvement — nitroglycerin may cause severe hypotension. Caution.
  • Anterior MI (V1–V4): Left anterior descending (LAD). Often largest infarction. "Widowmaker."
  • Lateral MI (I, aVL, V5–V6): Left circumflex (LCX).
  • Your job: acquire the 12-lead, transmit it to the receiving facility, do NOT delay transport to interpret
  • A transmitted pre-hospital 12-lead saves 20–30 minutes door-to-balloon time in STEMI
🧠 Hospital Analogy

Think of the 12-lead as the triage report you'd hand to the ER doc before a code. You're not making the final call — you're giving the team a head start. A pre-hospital 12-lead with STEMI identified = the cath lab is spinning up before the patient clears the ambulance bay doors.

NREMT-Style Assessment
Practice Question Bank — 60 Questions
Expanded to 60 scenario-based questions across 7 domains including the new ECG section.
Q1
Airway • O₂ Delivery
A 67-year-old with COPD. SpO₂ 88%, RR 26, accessory muscle use. Which O₂ device?
ANasal cannula at 2 LPM
BNon-rebreather at 15 LPM
CBVM at 15 LPM
DBlow-by only
✓ Correct: B — NRB at 15 LPM

SpO₂ 88% = hypoxia requiring treatment. Apply NRB. COPD hypoxic drive concern is real but NEVER prevents treating active hypoxia. Monitor and titrate as condition improves.

Q2
Airway • OPA/NPA
You insert an OPA and the patient immediately gags. Next action?
APush OPA further — not seated correctly
BRemove OPA and insert NPA
CSuction through OPA
DHold until gagging subsides
✓ Correct: B — Remove OPA, insert NPA

Gagging = intact gag reflex = OPA contraindicated. Remove immediately (risk of vomiting/aspiration). NPA is tolerated with gag reflex present. Rule: OPA = no gag, NPA = gag present.

Q3
Airway • Epiglottitis
4-year-old: high fever, drooling, tripod, "hot potato" voice, won't open mouth. Action?
AInsert tongue depressor to visualize
BInsert NPA to maintain airway
CPosition of comfort, blow-by O₂, transport emergently
DBegin BVM ventilations
✓ Correct: C — Position of comfort, blow-by O₂, emergency transport

Epiglottitis. ANY stimulation (tongue blade, NPA, laying flat) can trigger complete obstruction and arrest. Allow position of comfort. Blow-by O₂ if tolerated. ALS intercept. Immediate transport.

Q4
Airway • BVM Technique
During BVM ventilation, the abdomen rises instead of the chest. This indicates?
ANormal — confirms diaphragmatic breathing
BIncrease tidal volume
CEsophageal ventilation — reposition airway and re-seal mask
DInsert larger OPA
✓ Correct: C — Reposition and re-seal

Abdominal rise = esophageal ventilation (inflating stomach). Risk: aspiration. Reposition with head-tilt chin-lift, re-seal mask, deliver slow 1-second breaths watching for CHEST rise only.

Q5
Airway • Suction
SpO₂ drops from 94% to 86% during oropharyngeal suctioning. Priority action?
AContinue — clearing airway will improve SpO₂
BStop, ventilate with BVM until SpO₂ recovers, then resume if needed
CInsert NPA while suctioning continues
DIncrease suction pressure
✓ Correct: B — Stop, ventilate, resume

Max suction: 15 sec adults, 10 sec peds. Prolonged suctioning removes O₂ and causes vagal bradycardia. SpO₂ drop = STOP, ventilate with BVM until recovery, then resume in controlled intervals.

Q6
Airway • Opioid OD
55F unresponsive in car. RR 6, SpO₂ 82%, pinpoint pupils. Priority intervention?
AApply NRB and transport
BBVM ventilations immediately, then naloxone per protocol
CCheck blood glucose first
DStart CPR and apply AED
✓ Correct: B — BVM first, then Narcan

Opioid toxidrome: miosis + AMS + respiratory depression. SpO₂ 82% + RR 6 = respiratory failure. BVM corrects hypoxia in seconds. Narcan takes 2–3 min. Ventilate first.

Q7
Airway • CPAP
68M with CHF: pink frothy sputum, bilateral crackles, RR 34, SpO₂ 86%. He is sitting upright and alert. Best airway intervention?
ANasal cannula at 4 LPM
BNRB at 15 LPM, lay supine to improve venous return
CCPAP at 5–10 cmH₂O, maintain upright position
DBVM ventilations — RR 34 indicates respiratory failure
✓ Correct: C — CPAP upright

Classic pulmonary edema/CHF. CPAP is ideal: positive pressure splints alveoli open, reduces work of breathing, and pushes edema fluid back into capillaries. Upright position reduces preload. NRB is second-best if CPAP not available. NEVER lay flat — worsens pulmonary edema.

Q8
Airway • Tension PTX
MVA victim: unilateral absent breath sounds, tracheal deviation to the RIGHT, JVD, BP 74/42, HR 134. The trachea is deviated toward which side?
AThe RIGHT side — trachea always deviates toward the tension PTX
BThe RIGHT side — trachea deviates AWAY from the tension PTX (LEFT side affected)
CTracheal deviation is an unreliable finding in the field
DThe LEFT side — confirming left tension PTX
✓ Correct: B — Trachea deviates AWAY from tension PTX

Tension PTX: air accumulates on the affected side, pushing the mediastinum (including trachea) away from the tension side. Trachea deviating RIGHT = tension PTX on the LEFT. Tracheal deviation is a LATE and often subtle finding. Don't wait for it. Absent unilateral BS + JVD + hypotension = act. ALS/needle decompression. Rapid transport.

Q9
Cardiology • AED
AED says "No shock recommended" for an unresponsive, pulseless, apneic adult. Next action?
ACheck pulse again — AED must be wrong
BResume CPR immediately and transport
CWithhold CPR and wait for re-analysis
DDeliver a precordial thump
✓ Correct: B — Resume CPR immediately

"No shock advised" = asystole or PEA. Non-shockable. Resume high-quality CPR immediately. ALS to treat reversible causes (H's and T's). Minimize hands-off time.

Q10
Cardiology • Atypical AMI
72-year-old diabetic woman: "bad indigestion" at rest × 3 hours, diaphoretic, BP 128/82, SpO₂ 96%. No chest pain. What do you suspect?
AGI emergency — treat symptomatically
BAMI — 324mg aspirin, emergency transport, ALS intercept
CStable angina — assist with nitro if prescribed
DDiabetic emergency — check BGL
✓ Correct: B — Treat as AMI

Atypical AMI. Diabetics + women + elderly frequently lack classic chest pain due to autonomic neuropathy. "Indigestion" + diaphoresis + onset at rest = AMI until proven otherwise. 324mg aspirin. Emergency transport. Pre-notify for STEMI activation.

Q11
Cardiology • Anaphylaxis
45M bee sting 10 min ago: confused, BP 82/50, HR 130, hives, stridor. Priority treatment?
AHigh-flow O₂, supine, legs elevated, transport
BEpinephrine 0.3 mg IM, high-flow O₂, transport
CDiphenhydramine (Benadryl) first, then monitor
DALS only — EMT scope does not include anaphylaxis
✓ Correct: B — Epinephrine first

Anaphylactic shock. Epi reverses bronchoconstriction (β2) AND vasoconstriction (α1) simultaneously. Antihistamines are too slow. Epi first, then O₂, then ALS intercept and transport.

Q12
Cardiology • Nitroglycerin
Chest pain patient: BP 86/52, took sildenafil (Viagra) 6 hours ago. Patient wants his nitro. Action?

DDeliver a second shock while the rhythm is still organizing
✓ Correct: B — Resume CPR immediately

After defibrillation: resume CPR IMMEDIATELY for 2 minutes. The myocardium is stunned post-shock — it may generate electrical activity without effective mechanical output (i.e., PEA). Two minutes of CPR allows the heart to organize mechanically. After 2 minutes of CPR, pause briefly (<10 sec) to check rhythm AND pulse together. If pulse present and organized rhythm → post-resuscitation care. If VF recurs → shock again.

Q60
ECG • Value of Pre-Hospital 12-Lead
You acquire a 12-lead ECG showing anterior STEMI and transmit it to the receiving hospital while en route. Why does this matter clinically?
AIt allows the hospital to bill for the ECG separately
BIt allows the cath lab to activate before the patient arrives, saving 20–30 min door-to-balloon time
CIt allows the EMT to make the STEMI diagnosis and start definitive treatment
DIt is required by law before transporting AMI patients
✓ Correct: B — Cath lab activation before arrival

Pre-hospital 12-lead transmission is one of the highest-yield interventions an EMT/paramedic can perform for a STEMI patient. Every minute of occlusion = myocardium dying. A transmitted STEMI activates the catheterization lab team before the patient clears the ambulance bay — the door-to-balloon clock starts in the field, not the ED. Studies show 20–30+ minutes saved. That's cardiac muscle saved.
